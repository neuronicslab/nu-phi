    ν-Φ

    Biosignal acquisition for EEG and EMG research

    A 32-channel acquisition board with its own signal processing and edge ML. Record from electrodes, drive the inputs from a DAC, or stream simulated data from your PC, and build your processing on the board itself.

    Three ways to get signals in¶

    Electrodes¶

    Connect surface electrodes for EEG or EMG. Both use the same front end: the ADS1299 is a general biopotential converter, and the difference between an EEG and an EMG setup is gain, sample rate and montage, which you set from the PC.

    A DAC or signal generator¶

    Drive the analog inputs with a known signal to check the whole chain, from amplifier to result. The board also has an internal test signal and a shorted-input mode for measuring its own noise.

    Simulated data from your PC¶

    Stream recorded or synthetic data from the PC straight into the board's processing, with no electrodes connected. Useful for developing an algorithm, or for measuring a model's accuracy on a known dataset.

    What runs on the board¶

    Signal chain¶

    Filtering, notch, DC removal, envelopes and band power run on the board in real time, next to acquisition. The reference app streams a 2–28 Hz filtered signal with a 50 Hz notch, and alpha, SSVEP and neurofeedback measures ten times a second.

    Edge ML¶

    Trained models run on the board and send their classifications to the PC with the data. An EEGNet model ported to the board reproduced the accuracy of its reference implementation.

    The processor has two cores. One handles acquisition and the links to the PC; the other runs the processing. A slow or busy algorithm cannot delay sampling, and the board reports dropped and missed samples at all times, so you can check that it didn't. How it works has the details.

    Who it is for¶

    Researchers¶

    Run recordings from a desktop app: choose a preset, check the board with its built-in tests, and record. Every run saves the raw data, the board's health, and the settings it was recorded with.

    Developers¶

    Write your own processing as an app on the board, in C/C++, against one documented interface. Test it on simulated data from the PC, then run it on live signals without changing the recording side.

    Status¶

    ν-Φ is developed at the NeuRonICS Lab, IISc. Features described here are tested on the hardware; planned work is on the roadmap. Documentation and the developer kit are available to approved users. Request access.
